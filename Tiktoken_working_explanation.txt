Start with the simplest possible idea
Imagine you're designing a compression system. You have this text to store:
aaabdaaabac
Storing every character individually is wasteful. You notice "aa" appears a lot. So you say — let's replace every "aa" with a new symbol, say "Z":
ZabdZabac        (aa → Z)
Now "Za" appears twice. Replace it with "Y":
YbdYbac          (Za → Y)
That's the core idea of BPE — Byte Pair Encoding. Find the most frequent pair, merge it into one symbol, repeat.

How OpenAI trained the tokenizer
They ran BPE on a massive corpus of internet text — billions of documents. The algorithm:

Start with every individual character as a token
Find the most frequently co-occurring pair across the entire corpus
Merge that pair into a new token
Repeat ~100,000 times

After 100,000 merges, you have a vocabulary of 100,000 tokens — the cl100k vocabulary.
The result? Frequent English words got merged all the way into single tokens early. Rare words never got fully merged, so they stay as fragments.

Let's trace your exact examples
unbelievable → un + believ + able
Why? Because in the training corpus:

"able" appears in thousands of words (capable, remarkable, notable...) → merged early into one token
"un" appears as a prefix constantly → merged early
"believ" is common enough → got its own token
But "unbelievable" as a whole word is rare enough that it never got a dedicated merge

ChatGPT → Chat + G + PT

"Chat" — very common English word → one token
"GPT" — this is the key insight. The tokenizer was trained before GPT was a household name. So "GPT" wasn't frequent enough to get merged as a unit. It stays as G + PT

... → 1 token
Three dots together appear constantly in internet text. Got merged into a single token very early.

The rule, stated simply

A sequence of characters becomes one token if it appeared together frequently enough during training to survive as a merged unit. Otherwise it gets broken into whatever sub-pieces did survive.

There's no grammar rule. No linguistic rule. Pure statistics over a massive corpus.
